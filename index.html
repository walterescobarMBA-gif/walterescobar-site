My Story

I was raised in Torrance, California, in a loving and stable family environment. That foundation taught me what stability gives a person: room to grow, to imagine, to recover, and to build.

Because I know what stability can make possible, I also understand what is lost when systems destabilize people who are already carrying too much.

My life has included hardship, but hardship did not narrow my vision. It sharpened it.

The Throughline

I grew up understanding stability.

I spent a decade inside regulated systems.

I experienced housing instability firsthand.

Now I translate that arc into accountability work.

Professional Foundation

From 2008 to 2018, I worked in the nonprofit financial sector through credit unions.

That decade trained me to understand compliance, documentation, internal controls, audit trails, member service, and the discipline required when institutions handle people’s stability.

When I look at a publicly funded housing program, I do not just see isolated mistakes. I see whether the system has controls, whether decisions are traceable, and whether the people being served are protected by the structure.

Purpose

My reason for doing this work is love for my community.

Housing advocacy is community protection work. It is how we defend dignity, safety, health, and possibility for people too often forced to survive systems that were not designed around their full humanity.

The LGBTQ+ community carries a history of survival, brilliance, loss, resistance, and unfinished work. I want my life and work to help carry that responsibility forward.

Core Thesis

Housing programs should stabilize people, not route them through unclear procedures, shifting expectations, and circular grievance systems.

If a program is funded to serve vulnerable communities, then clarity, dignity, cultural competence, and accountability should be built into the structure.

Academics & Public Policy

My education is part of the mission. Public policy gives language, structure, and strategy to what lived experience and professional systems work have already taught me.

I want to understand how programs are funded, governed, monitored, audited, corrected, and redesigned.

Leadership Philosophy

Lead with evidence. A strong record can move through rooms where emotion alone is dismissed.

Protect the community. Advocacy should be rooted in care, dignity, and refusal to let people be quietly failed.

Build what was missing. When systems do not provide clarity, create tools that make the path visible for others.
