My Story

I was raised in Torrance, California, in a loving and stable family environment. That foundation shaped how I understand safety, dignity, and possibility.

Stability is not abstract to me. It is the ground people need before they can heal, study, work, advocate, and build a life.

My life has included hardship, but hardship did not erase what I was given. It clarified what I want to protect for others.

The Throughline

I grew up understanding stability.

I worked inside regulated systems.

I experienced housing instability firsthand.

Now I am turning that arc into public accountability work.

Professional Foundation

From 2008 to 2018, I worked in the nonprofit financial sector through credit unions.

That decade trained me to understand rigid systems, compliance frameworks, documentation standards, internal controls, audit trails, member service, and process integrity.

When I look at a publicly funded housing program, I do not just see isolated mistakes. I see whether the system has controls, whether decisions are traceable, and whether the people being served are actually protected by the structure.

Purpose

My reason for doing this work is love for my community.

I believe housing advocacy is community protection work. It is how we defend dignity, safety, health, and possibility for people who are too often forced to survive systems that were not designed around their full humanity.

The LGBTQ+ community carries a history of survival, loss, resistance, brilliance, and unfinished work. I want my life and work to help carry that responsibility forward.

Core Thesis

Housing programs should stabilize people, not push them through unclear procedures, shifting expectations, and circular grievance routes.

If a program is funded to serve vulnerable communities, then clarity, dignity, cultural competence, and accountability should be built into the structure.

Academics & Public Policy

My education is part of the mission. Public policy gives language, structure, and strategy to what lived experience and professional systems work have already taught me.

I want to understand how programs are funded, governed, monitored, audited, corrected, and redesigned.

Leadership Philosophy

Lead with evidence. A strong record can move through rooms where emotion alone is dismissed.

Protect the community. Advocacy should be rooted in care, dignity, and refusal to let people be quietly failed.

Build what was missing. When systems do not provide clarity, create tools that make the path visible for others.
