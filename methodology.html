Methodology

How the project is built.

This page explains how records are collected, organized, reviewed, summarized, and separated from analysis within this public-interest housing systems project.

Research Approach

This project uses a participant-centered documentation method. It combines preserved communications, administrative notices, oversight records, public records productions, monitoring materials, and timeline reconstruction.

Method 01

Document Preservation

Notices, emails, records, directives, and communications are preserved to maintain chronology, context, and administrative sequence.

Method 02

Public Records Review

Public records requests are used to obtain policies, procedures, oversight materials, monitoring reports, and administrative guidance relevant to housing systems.

Method 03

Timeline Reconstruction

Events are organized chronologically to identify administrative sequence, operational changes, oversight responses, and stabilization-related developments.

The methodology is designed to turn fragmented experiences into structured documentation without overstating what the records show.

Fact vs. Analysis

The project distinguishes between documented facts, preserved records, administrative chronology, and interpretive analysis.

Documented Facts

Dates, notices, correspondence, directives, reports, and policy materials are treated as source records.

Interpretation

Analysis focuses on patterns, operational structure, stabilization concerns, and how administrative systems appear to function over time.

Limits

The project avoids unsupported legal conclusions, personal attacks, or claims that exceed what the available documentation can reasonably support.

Privacy & Controlled Disclosure

The project intentionally limits publication of sensitive, private, medical, identifying, or unnecessary personal information.

Selective Publication

Not every document is published. Some materials are summarized, withheld, redacted, or referenced only at a high level to preserve privacy and context.

Program Neutrality

Public language is intentionally framed around federally funded housing systems, stabilization, oversight, and administration rather than sensitive program identifiers.

Context Protection

Documentation is presented only where it supports public-interest understanding of systems, chronology, accountability, or stabilization-related concerns.

The goal is public understanding, not overexposure.

Source Categories

The project draws from several categories of records and materials.

Participant-Retained Records

Notices, emails, photographs, timeline notes, and preserved communications retained during the housing instability period.

Public Records Productions

Documents obtained through California Public Records Act requests, including policies, reports, directives, and administrative materials.

Oversight Materials

Guidance, monitoring records, correspondence, and administrative materials connected to housing stabilization systems.

Analytical Purpose

The project uses documentation to examine how housing systems operate when participants encounter instability, unclear authority, fragmented communication, or delayed stabilization.

The purpose is to preserve chronology, identify patterns, support public understanding, and contribute to more participant-centered housing systems analysis.
